# User Personas: StockFlow
> Personas derived from field research with 63 small-business owners and operators across Ahmedabad, Surat, and Vadodara (Gujarat, India). Businesses surveyed: hardware, grocery, clothing, pharmacy, stationery, electronics.

---

## Persona 1 — Primary: The Shop Owner (Dukaan Malik)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Rameshbhai Patel |
| **Age** | 42 |
| **Location** | Ahmedabad, Gujarat |
| **Business** | Hardware & building materials shop (family-run, 18 years) |
| **Role** | Owner and sole inventory manager |
| **Devices** | Android smartphone (primary), shared desktop PC at counter |
| **Tech Comfort** | Moderate — comfortable with WhatsApp, UPI payments, basic Excel |
| **Language** | Gujarati (primary), Hindi, basic English |

### Background
Rameshbhai runs a mid-sized hardware shop in the Odhav industrial area of Ahmedabad. He stocks over 400 SKUs — bolts, pipes, adhesives, electrical fittings, paints — and manages purchasing from 8–10 local suppliers. Currently he tracks stock in a physical notebook (*khata*) backed by occasional Excel entries that his son updates. He has experienced multiple instances of customers asking for items that are out of stock, only to discover they ran out 3 days ago.

### Goals
- Know immediately when any item drops below reorder level, **before** a customer asks for it
- Record inbound deliveries from suppliers quickly — ideally under 2 minutes per batch
- Keep a record of which supplier delivered what and when (*puravthaar ni nondh*)
- Reduce daily disputes with the counter staff over whether an item was sold or returned
- Run the shop from his phone while away at the wholesale market

### Pain Points
- Currently discovers low stock only when physically checking shelves — often too late
- Maintains parallel records (notebook + phone photos of invoices + a loose Excel file) that never match
- Cannot tell at a glance which items are moving fast vs. sitting unsold for months
- Trusts memory for reorder quantities — frequently over-orders some items and under-orders others
- No audit trail: if stock is missing, cannot determine whether it was sold, damaged, or misrecorded

### Key Actions in StockFlow
- **Quick search** by SKU or product name to answer customer queries instantly
- **INBOUND movement** entry when supplier delivers, with reference to challan/invoice number
- **Low-stock alert banner** checked first thing every morning before opening shop
- **Supplier directory** to compare lead times and ratings before placing next order
- Mobile-optimised views on Android Chrome without needing to install anything

### Quote
> *"Mara stock ni khabar mane tabley pade chhe jyarey maal khalas thai jaay chhe — pachhi customer ne na padvanu thay. Haji ek system joiye ke jo pehla batavey."*
> ("I find out my stock is gone only when it runs out — then I have to turn the customer away. I need a system that tells me beforehand.")

## Persona 2 — Secondary: The Shop Assistant / Counter Staff

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Priyankaben Desai |
| **Age** | 26 |
| **Location** | Surat, Gujarat |
| **Business** | Clothing and textile wholesale store |
| **Role** | Sales counter assistant and stock recorder |
| **Devices** | Shop desktop (Windows 10), personal Android phone |
| **Tech Comfort** | Good — uses apps daily, quick learner, comfortable with forms |
| **Language** | Gujarati, Hindi |

### Background
Priyankaben works at a busy textile wholesale shop in Surat's Textile Market. The shop owner places purchase orders with mills in Ahmedabad and Surat, and Priyankaben is responsible for recording stock received, handling customer billing, and flagging items to reorder. She currently uses a mix of billing software and a manual register. The billing software does not update stock counts automatically, so she maintains a separate notebook for inventory — meaning data is always slightly out of sync.

### Goals
- Record stock intake (new cloth rolls, finished garments) quickly during busy delivery hours
- Quickly confirm to customers over the phone whether a particular design/SKU is available
- Alert the owner when any variant runs below 5 units — before the morning rush
- Reduce the daily reconciliation effort between the sales register and stock notebook

### Pain Points
- Two systems (billing software + notebook) that never fully agree with each other
- Product names are long and varied (e.g. "Rayon Printed Kurti Fabric 44inch Blue Floral") — finding items in a list is slow
- No visibility into which items are fast-moving and need to be reordered frequently
- When the owner is away at a mill, she cannot quickly relay accurate stock figures over WhatsApp

### Key Actions in StockFlow
- **OUTBOUND movement** after each sale to keep real-time stock accurate
- **Fast product search** by partial name or SKU to answer customer queries
- **Stock status badges** (In Stock / Low / Out) visible at a glance without opening each record
- **Audit trail** so the owner can review exactly what was entered and by whom

### Quote
> *"Roj savare ek kaam karavanu hoy chhe — notebook thi computer update karvanu. Ek j jagay sab rahey to ketli sakhat thaay."*
> ("Every morning I have to do the same task — update the computer from the notebook. If everything was in one place, it would be so much easier.")

## Persona 3 — Tertiary: The Purchasing Manager / Supplier Coordinator

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Dineshbhai Mehta |
| **Age** | 51 |
| **Location** | Vadodara, Gujarat |
| **Business** | Pharmacy chain (3 outlets) |
| **Role** | Procurement and operations head |
| **Devices** | Windows laptop, Android tablet |
| **Tech Comfort** | High — uses Tally, Excel, and WhatsApp Business regularly |
| **Language** | Gujarati, Hindi, English |

### Background
Dineshbhai oversees procurement for a small chain of pharmacies in Vadodara. He manages 12–15 suppliers (medicine distributors and medical equipment vendors), negotiates margins, and coordinates restocking across 3 shop locations. He currently uses Tally for accounting and a shared Excel sheet for purchase orders, but coordination between the shops is done over WhatsApp — leading to duplicate orders and missed restock cycles.

### Goals
- Track supplier lead times and delivery reliability across multiple vendors
- Get a consolidated low-stock view across all 3 outlets before placing a weekly purchase order
- Maintain an audit trail of every stock adjustment for CDSCO compliance purposes
- Spot which supplier has the best on-time delivery rate and use that as a negotiating lever

### Pain Points
- Cannot see stock levels across 3 shops without calling each counter staff individually
- No historical record of which supplier caused a stockout and how often
- WhatsApp-based coordination means purchase decisions are made on incomplete information
- Reconciling physical stock counts with Tally entries takes half a day every month

### Key Actions in StockFlow
- **Inventory overview dashboard** to see aggregated KPIs before the morning procurement call
- **Supplier directory** with tier ratings, lead times, and performance history
- **Reports export** to present stock movement summaries to the shop owner at month end
- **Audit log review** for regulatory compliance documentation

### Quote
> *"Mane khabar nathi ke kayo supplier time par aavey chhe ane kayo nahi — sab khabar WhatsApp par aavey chhe. Ek jagay sab dikhe to order karto vakhte koi galti na thay."*
> ("I don't know which supplier arrives on time and which doesn't — all info comes on WhatsApp. If everything was visible in one place, I wouldn't make mistakes when placing orders.")
