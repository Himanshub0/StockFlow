# Empathy Maps: StockFlow
> Two empathy maps — one for the shop owner persona (Rameshbhai), one for the counter staff persona (Priyankaben). Based on field interviews and survey responses from 63 small business owners and staff in Gujarat.

---

## Empathy Map 1 — Rameshbhai Patel (Shop Owner, Hardware, Ahmedabad)

### 💬 Says
- *"Maal aavshe tyarey stock update karisu."* ("I'll update the stock when the goods arrive.")
- *"Khata maa lakhyu chhe pan computer maa update nathi thyu."* ("It's written in the ledger but not updated in the computer.")
- *"Customer puche chhe, mane khabar nathi ke maal chhe ke nahi."* ("The customer is asking, and I don't know if the item is in stock.")
- "I found out we ran out of cement adhesive only when a customer complained."
- "I don't need anything fancy — just tell me what is finishing."
- "If I could check stock from my phone while I'm at the market, that would be enough."

### 🤔 Thinks
- *"Aaj kem delivery nahi aayi? Supplier ne phone karo?"* ("Why hasn't the delivery come today? Should I call the supplier?")
- "What if my staff entered the wrong quantity again?"
- "I ordered 50 bags last month — why do we only have 12 now?"
- "If this item finishes during the festival season, customers will go to the competitor across the street."
- "I should have reordered this last week — I keep forgetting."
- "My son knows the computer but I can't always call him just for stock checks."

### 🏃 Does
- Walks the shop floor each morning and visually checks shelf levels for high-selling items
- Calls or WhatsApps the supplier directly when he suspects something is running low
- Writes deliveries in a physical khata (ledger) — sometimes updates the computer later, sometimes forgets
- Relies on memory or old purchase bills to estimate how much to reorder
- Photographs invoices with his phone to remember delivery details
- Asks counter staff to inform him if any item gets "last few pieces"

### ❤️ Feels
- **Anxious** during wedding/festival season when demand spikes unpredictably
- **Embarrassed** when he cannot answer a customer query about stock availability
- **Relieved** on days when no stockouts are reported before shop closing
- **Frustrated** when manual records don't match physical stock and he has to investigate
- **Confident** when he has a full view of what's in the godown vs. what's on the shelf
- **Curious** about whether a digital system will be too complicated or require too much maintenance

## Empathy Map 2 — Priyankaben Desai (Counter Staff, Clothing, Surat)

### 💬 Says
- "The billing software shows one number and my notebook shows something different — I go by the notebook."
- "Every time a new roll comes in I have to write it in three places."
- "Customers call and ask if a particular design is available — I have to physically go check."
- *"Saheb bahar gaya chhe, to mane khabar nathi ketlu stock chhe."* ("The owner has gone out, so I don't know how much stock is there.")
- "I want something simple — not like Tally which requires training."
- "When I make a mistake in the entry, there's no way to fix it without crossing out in the register."

### 🤔 Thinks
- "Did I already record this delivery or not? The notebook entry looks like it's from yesterday."
- "If the owner sees the wrong stock count in the system he'll think I didn't do my work."
- "I wish I could just scan the barcode instead of typing the product name every time."
- "What if I accidentally enter 100 instead of 10 — will it mess everything up?"
- "I spend the first 30 minutes every morning just reconciling last night's entries."

### 🏃 Does
- Maintains a physical notebook with incoming stock entries alongside the billing software
- Calls or sends a WhatsApp message to the owner when she notices any item is low
- Looks up items by scrolling through long printed lists or visually scanning shelves
- Manually reconciles the sales register with stock entries at the end of every day
- Double-checks received quantities against the supplier's challan (delivery slip)

### ❤️ Feels
- **Stressed** during delivery days when multiple suppliers arrive at the same time and she has to record everything simultaneously
- **Confused** when the two systems (billing + notebook) disagree and she doesn't know which to trust
- **Proud** when the end-of-day reconciliation balances perfectly
- **Relieved** when a product search works instantly and she can answer the customer's call in seconds
- **Motivated** to do the job well but held back by tools that create more work than they save
